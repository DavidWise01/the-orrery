honest physics

Orbits, not a crash

You asked for gravity, and the honest version matters. A real n‑body simulation of a sun plus 37 masses is chaotic — within seconds something slingshots off‑screen or falls into the sun. That's not gravity working, that's gravity unsolvable in closed form. So every body here rides a stable Keplerian orbit — the exact two‑body solution, which is what real orbits are — with the inner planets moving faster than the outer ones (Kepler's third law, ω ∝ R^-1.5). It looks like gravity because it is gravity, just the part that stays on the screen.

And the sun's photons: radiation pressure is real, but for a planet it's about ten‑trillion times weaker than the sun's gravity — it never pushes a planet away. It does push dust, which is why comet tails always point away from the sun. So here the photons stream out and blow the dust outward, while the planets stay held by gravity. That's the truthful behavior — I pushed back on "the sun blows the planets away" because it doesn't.
